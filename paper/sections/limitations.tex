\section{Limitations}

This study evaluates one quantized model and one prompting pipeline, so the
results do not establish a general ranking of model families. The source data
is English rather than naturally occurring Romanized Indic input, and the
experiment therefore measures controlled translation and romanization.

The current evaluation contains no native-speaker ratings. Automatic
cross-lingual similarity cannot establish fluency, register appropriateness,
cultural fit, or standardized transliteration quality. Raw multilingual
BERTScore is particularly difficult to interpret across English and Romanized
Indic text because its token alignment is sensitive to surface vocabulary.

English-copy outputs are treated as failures and assigned zero across the row.
Blank or malformed fields also receive zero for the affected metric. This
failure-aware policy is intentionally strict and may conflate infrastructure
failures with linguistic failures. For duplicate Telugu reruns, we retain the
first generation; a different selection rule could produce slightly different
aggregates. All experiments should still be rerun from a clean manifest before
submission.

Finally, romanization has no single universally correct spelling. Future work
should evaluate multiple acceptable forms and recruit native speakers from
different regions and demographic backgrounds.
