# Romanization Evaluation Analysis

## Metrics

- **Raw BERTScore F1:** Calculated with `bert-base-multilingual-cased` and
  `rescale_with_baseline=False`.
- **BGE cosine similarity:** Cosine similarity between English and romanized
  text embeddings generated by Ollama `bge-m3`.
- Blank or failed generations are excluded from the means.

## Results

| Language | Query BERTScore F1 | Query cosine | Response BERTScore F1 | Response cosine | Query rows | Response rows |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hindi | 0.649961 | 0.762160 | 0.663384 | 0.812388 | 142 | 142 |
| Telugu | 0.721319 | 0.802233 | 0.745926 | 0.834186 | 118 | 115 |
| Kannada | 0.639757 | 0.701474 | 0.759933 | 0.819542 | 142 | 103 |

## Analysis

Telugu has the strongest overall semantic results. It has the highest query
BERTScore F1, query cosine similarity, and response cosine similarity. Its
response BERTScore F1 is also strong.

Kannada has the highest response BERTScore F1, but this mean covers only 103 of
142 responses. Because 39 blank or failed responses were excluded, the Kannada
response result may be biased upward and should not be treated as the strongest
overall result without evaluating the missing rows.

Hindi has complete coverage across all 142 query and response rows. Its scores
are lower than Telugu's, but its averages are the most directly representative
of the entire dataset.

For this cross-lingual comparison, BGE-M3 cosine similarity should be treated as
the primary semantic metric. Raw BERTScore F1 is useful as a secondary metric,
but English and romanized Indian-language text do not share the same vocabulary,
which can depress or distort token-alignment scores.

## Conclusion

Based primarily on BGE-M3 cosine similarity, the current quality ranking is:

1. Telugu
2. Kannada
3. Hindi

Coverage must be reported alongside these scores, especially for Kannada and
Telugu.
