\section{Conclusion}

We evaluated four LLMs---Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, and Qwen3.5-35B---on
romanized Hindi, Telugu, and Kannada generation under a failure-aware scoring
policy that assigns zero to empty, copied, and script-corrupted outputs. Three
findings stand out. First, language is a stronger predictor of output quality
than model choice: all four models follow the same Hindi $>$ Telugu $>$ Kannada
ordering, consistent with the relative availability of romanized Indic text in
pretraining data. Second, Gemini and Ollama exhibit non-overlapping failure
modes---script corruption and instruction collapse respectively---that are
invisible without explicit coverage tracking, and that failure-aware scoring
exposes by materially changing model rankings. Third, and most importantly,
BGE-M3 cosine similarity assigns scores in the 0.73--0.84 range across all
language conditions for the two strongest models (Anthropic and GPT-4o),
including outputs that return pure English verbatim (BGE = 1.000) and outputs
rendered entirely in native script (BGE $\approx$ 0.82). Native-speaker human evaluation reveals systematic quality
failures---unnatural register, wrong word choice, inconsistent romanization---
that this band conceals. The metric cannot see the gap that users experience.

Romanized generation for Indic languages requires evaluation frameworks that
combine semantic metrics with coverage, script compliance, and native-speaker
judgment. We release our scoring pipeline, the failure-aware evaluation
protocol, and the human annotation rubric to support future benchmarking of
this underserved but large-scale user population.
