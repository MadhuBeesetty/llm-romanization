\begin{abstract}
Romanized Latin script is widely used by speakers of Indic languages, yet
open-ended generation in this representation remains poorly evaluated. We
benchmark Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, and Qwen3.5-35B on converting 142 English
query--response pairs into romanized Hindi, Telugu, and Kannada. Outputs are
evaluated using multilingual BERTScore and BGE-M3 cosine similarity under a
failure-aware policy that assigns zero to empty outputs, English copies,
native-script leakage, and repetition loops. GPT-4o and Claude achieve the
strongest response-level BGE-M3 scores, ranging from 0.733 to 0.837 across the
three languages. However, aggregate semantic scores obscure distinct failure
modes. Gemini exhibits script leakage, affecting 24 Telugu and 19 Kannada
responses, while Qwen frequently returns English unchanged and produces
repetition loops. Verbatim English outputs can receive perfect similarity
scores before failure-aware correction, demonstrating that semantic similarity
alone does not measure successful romanization. Performance is consistently
strongest for Hindi and weaker for Telugu and Kannada. These findings show that
romanized Indic generation should be evaluated using semantic similarity
together with completion reliability, script compliance, English-copy
detection, and repetition analysis.
\end{abstract}
