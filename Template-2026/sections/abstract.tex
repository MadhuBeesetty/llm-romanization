\begin{abstract}
Hundreds of millions of South Asian language speakers interact with AI systems
in romanized Latin script---typing their native language with QWERTY keyboards
rather than native characters---yet no standard benchmark evaluates LLM
generation quality in this representation. We evaluate four LLMs (Claude,
GPT-4o, Gemini, and Qwen3.5-35B) on romanized Hindi, Telugu, and Kannada
generation using 142 English query--response pairs from the OpenAssistant
dataset. Outputs are scored with multilingual BERTScore and BGE-M3 cosine
similarity under a failure-aware policy that assigns zero to empty, copied, and
native-script-corrupted responses rather than excluding them. Automatic scores
range from 0.76 to 0.84 across all model and language combinations---a band
that appears acceptable. Native-speaker human evaluation reveals systematic
quality failures invisible to these metrics: unnatural register, wrong word
choice, and inconsistent romanization that native speakers consistently reject.
The divergence between automatic and human judgment is the central finding:
current evaluation frameworks cannot see the gap that users experience.
\end{abstract}
